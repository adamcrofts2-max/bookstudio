# Book Studio — End-to-End UX & Functionality Audit

**Date:** 31 July 2026
**Method:** Live testing in Chrome against the deployed build (bookstudio-rose.vercel.app), using a real 16-chapter manuscript project ("test5" — *Forest Gardening for Beginners*) with a genuine pre-designed cover image already in place. Every area below was clicked, typed into, and (where destructive) undone and verified reverted — this is not a code read, it's what a real author would hit using the app today.

---

## What works well

**Chapters sidebar.** Add, reorder, and delete all behave correctly and cleanly. Reordering moves the right chapter, content stays attached to its chapter, and nothing was left in a broken state after repeated test edits.

**Content editing.** The sidebar paragraph editor, the block hover toolbar (move up/down, duplicate, page-break toggle, delete), and the manual page-break toggle all did exactly what they claimed, and Undo correctly reverted a page-break test change — confirmed by re-checking the block state after.

**Structural pages & cover designer.** This is a genuinely strong part of the app. The cover designer's click-to-upload, focal point/zoom, overlay controls, and — notably — the text-visibility toggle are all present and working; the test project's cover is a real example of an author using that toggle correctly (title/subtitle/author are baked into the uploaded image, and the app's own overlay text is switched to Hidden so nothing doubles up).

**Inspector — Page, Type, Image, Notes tabs.** All four are consistent, show the right context for whatever's selected, and nothing felt broken or half-wired.

**Dark mode.** Correctly scoped — the app chrome (toolbar, sidebars) darkens, but the page canvas itself stays print-accurate light. This is the right call for a WYSIWYG book editor and it's easy to get wrong.

**Export.** The Export dropdown offers exactly three clearly-labelled options — PDF (print-ready, bleed & crop marks), EPUB (reflowable ebook), HTML (single-file web book) — each with a one-line explanation of what it's for. No ambiguity about what you're about to generate.

**Keyboard Shortcuts and Version History dialogs.** Both are clean, calmly written, and do what they say (shortcuts list is accurate; version history explains the autosave/manual-save distinction clearly).

**Virtual Editor.** This is the standout feature of the app. Running "Review Entire Book" against the real 16-chapter, ~30,800-word manuscript produced genuinely useful, specific findings — not generic filler: an unbalanced bracket in Chapter Nine, an inconsistent capitalisation of "Forest Gardening" vs. "forest gardening" across the book, nine readability flags for overlong sentences in specific chapters, a missing Copyright page, ISBN page, Back Cover blurb, Title Page and author bio, two unreplaced image placeholders called out by chapter name, and four chapters whose titles break the book's own "Chapter One:"-style numbering convention. Every finding has a confidence score, a plain-English "why it matters," and Reject / Edit / Ignore / Ignore-similar actions. Accepted fixes go through a real revision log with per-fix Restore, tied to the same `contentStore.updateBlock` path as manual edits — nothing is silently overwritten. For a feature the app itself admits is only partly built ("Proofreading is real today; the rest of the taxonomy is designed and lands incrementally"), what's live already outperforms what most standalone grammar tools offer, because it understands book structure rather than just prose.

---

## What could be improved

**Chapters sidebar icon crowding.** The four action icons added for reordering (move up/down, rename, delete) now crowd the chapter title text in the Chapters tab — on hover, titles truncate and become hard to read. This reads as a regression from the recent reorder feature rather than a deliberate design choice, and is worth a layout pass (e.g. reveal icons only on row hover, or move them into an overflow menu).

**Inspector's "Theme" tab is dead and misleading.** It still shows a placeholder reading "Theme editing arrives in Phase 4," but a full, working Theme Gallery already exists — just one level up, in Project Settings. A real user has no way to know that; they'll click the Inspector's own Theme tab, see a "not built yet" message, and reasonably conclude the app can't change themes at all. Either wire this tab to open the real gallery, or remove the tab until it's connected.

**Inspector tab row overflow.** At default zoom/window width, the five Inspector tabs (Page/Type/Image/Notes/Theme) genuinely overflow their fixed-width panel — confirmed on both edges: "Theme" truncates to "Th"/"Tl" on the right, and after a horizontal scroll, "FRONT MATTER" truncates to "NT MATTER" on the left. This isn't a one-off rendering glitch; it's a real layout constraint worth fixing given the project's own design-system rule about generous, non-cluttered spacing.

**Virtual Editor's two-step dependency isn't explained until after you hit the wall.** Layout and Publishing Quality scores stay stuck on "Not yet analysed" until the manuscript view has rendered at least once in the session — the app tells you this, but only in a small caption next to the Review button, after you've already run a review and gotten partial results. A first-time user is likely to just see two scores permanently blank and assume the feature is broken.

**Performance on large manuscripts.** Both the manuscript canvas and the Virtual Editor's full-book review repeatedly froze the browser tab's renderer for 30+ seconds at a time during this session, on a real but modest 16-chapter project — this happened independently, more than once, and matches the project's own `ROADMAP.md` entry flagging a structural-page mutation freeze on large projects. Given this hit a genuinely normal-sized manuscript rather than an extreme stress case, it's worth treating as a near-term priority rather than a background item.

~~**Version History shows zero autosaved versions despite a long real editing session.**~~ **Correction, same day:** checked the code — this isn't a bug. `useAutosaveSnapshots.ts` runs a real 5-minute timer, correctly skipping a tick if nothing changed since the last one. This audit session repeatedly recreated Chrome tabs to work around unrelated CDP screenshot timeouts, which reloads the app and restarts that 5-minute timer from zero each time — no single tab ran uninterrupted long enough for a tick to fire. Not an app issue.

## What's still missing or untested

The Virtual Editor's own Commercial Quality check flags this specific book for having no Copyright page, ISBN page, Back Cover blurb, Title Page, or author bio — that's a gap in the test manuscript's content, not necessarily a missing app feature, but it's a fair proxy for how easy the app makes it to notice and fill these gaps. Right now that discovery only happens if you run a full Virtual Editor review; there's no lighter-weight nudge (e.g. a checklist on the Structure tab) pointing new users toward the standard front/back matter a "properly published" book needs before it's this deep into writing.

A few areas weren't reached this session and are worth a follow-up pass: the Back Cover designer specifically (only the front Cover was exercised in depth), the Save/Load buttons' actual file-write/file-read behaviour (opened but not click-tested end-to-end), and the Assets tab in the left sidebar (visible but not opened).

---

## Bottom line

The core editing loop — write, structure, style, export — is solid and the things a working author touches most (chapters, content blocks, cover design) all held up under real use with no data loss. The two Inspector issues (dead Theme tab, tab overflow) are quick, high-value fixes since they actively mislead rather than just look rough. The performance freeze on a normal-sized project is the one item here that would visibly hurt a real author mid-session and is worth pulling forward. The Virtual Editor is ahead of where the rest of the audit would predict — it's the part of the app most likely to make someone say "this is better than I expected," and doubling down on it (once the Layout/Publishing Quality categories land) is probably the highest-leverage direction for differentiating Book Studio from Vellum/Atticus.
